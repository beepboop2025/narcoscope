Drug precursor incident evidence—and what it cannot prove

NarcoScope’s cited analysis uses the public INCB Precursors Report 2025 to describe selected official incidents. It keeps enforcement observations, lawful-trade capabilities, restricted systems and overdose mortality in separate evidence lanes because their populations, units and identifiers do not join.

What the public record supports

Documented incidentsSelected regulatory and enforcement observations in a public annual report.
Exact attribution“Reported origin” is preserved as reported origin, never converted into guilt or cause.
Visible missing joinsNo invented link to lawful trade volumes, private systems, court records or deaths.

The published investigation reports the INCB document’s selected incident aggregate—nine reported incidents and nearly five tonnes—while preserving the report’s less-than precision. It does not represent that aggregate as one shipment or as a census of all precursor trade or diversion.

Questions the evidence can—and cannot—answer

What is a precursor incident record?

It is a regulatory or enforcement observation about a chemical or related material. The annual public report contains selected events, while operational PICS and PEN Online records are restricted to authorized users and are not part of this public build.

What does “China-linked” mean here?

Only that the INCB report says substances reportedly originated in China. It is not a finding about the conduct of a country, industry, company or person, and the public paragraph does not supply every bilateral allocation.

Can these incidents be linked to overdose deaths?

No causal link is available in the checked inputs. CDC mortality and INCB incidents share no incident, shipment, exporter or case identifier. NarcoScope can place them in separate panels but does not pretend that proximity is attribution.

Critical denominator: public merchandise-trade systems can provide lawful aggregate trade context, but NarcoScope has not admitted a matching chemical-product series into this dossier. Without that denominator, the incident extract cannot measure the share of trade that was lawful, diverted or intercepted.

Audit the analysis

  • Read the public story’s 28 sentence-level citations and exact PDF page/paragraph locators.
  • Inspect the machine brief and cited dossier rather than relying on a summary.
  • Keep “reported origin,” “incident,” “seizure,” “shipment” and “trade” as different terms.
  • Do not infer national responsibility, intent or a mortality pathway without case-level evidence.
